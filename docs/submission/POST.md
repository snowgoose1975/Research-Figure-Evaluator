## Suggested text

What does an AI reader take away from a scientific figure?

I built Figure Relay, an exploratory tool that makes that interpretation visible. Reader A sees only the original image and saves structured notes. A deterministic renderer reconstructs an image from those notes, and Reader B reads only that reconstruction. The author’s intended messages are withheld from both readers and compared with their saved records afterward.

Upload a public figure, write three to five intended messages, and inspect the two readings, cited evidence, and reconstruction. Reports can be saved and reopened in the same browser. The website also includes recorded cases from published research papers.

A historical local Qwen3-VL 4B protocol completed 8 of 12 attempts on four figures across physics, materials, and mathematics; four Reader A output-limit failures were retained. Completion is not accuracy. The experiments exposed lost geometry, changed reference-line readings, and model mistakes. Current development uploads still reveal arrow-reading and repeated-label limitations.

This is an early review aid, not a measure of human understanding or scientific correctness. Differences can come from the reader, the reconstruction, or the second reading; their cause is not automatically established. Free-text assessments need human review.

Try it: https://figure-relay.chicchair5.chatgpt.site
Public code and write-up: https://github.com/snowgoose1975/Research-Figure-Evaluator

The new-image trial runs on my computer while it is online and accepting requests. Only upload already-public figures you are authorized to share. You can also run the project locally with a supported model endpoint.
